Section 01

What Is This Project?

A jargon-free explanation of what the project does, why it exists, and who it is for.

The Simple Version

Stock markets don't behave the same way every day. Sometimes they go up consistently for months (a Bull market). Sometimes they crash and fall rapidly (a Bear market). And sometimes they just move sideways, going nowhere in particular (Sideways market).

This project is a computer system that automatically detects which of these three conditions the market is currently in — and then tells you how to split your money across 10 different investments to get the best return for the least risk in that specific condition.

🤔 The Problem

Investors and fund managers lose money by using the same strategy in all market conditions. A strategy that works in a Bull market often loses big during a Bear market.

💡 The Solution

Use machine learning (specifically a Hidden Markov Model) to detect the current market regime from data — then optimize the portfolio specifically for that regime.

📊 The Data

15+ years of daily price data for 10 major ETFs, plus economic indicators (VIX fear index, yield curve, CPI inflation, unemployment) from the US Federal Reserve.

🎯 The Goal

Beat a simple "buy and hold SPY" strategy by being smart about when to be aggressive (Bull) versus defensive (Bear) with your investments.

Why Regimes Matter — A Real-World Analogy

🌦️ Think of it like weather forecasting for money Just like you dress differently depending on the weather — rain gear for storms, light clothes for sunny days — a smart investor allocates money differently depending on market "weather." This project is the weather forecast system. It tells you today's market climate so you can dress (invest) appropriately.

The Three Market Regimes

🐂
Bull

Markets rising steadily. Low fear. Strong momentum. Best time to hold growth stocks like SPY, QQQ, IWM.

🐻
Bear

Markets falling. High fear (VIX spike). Negative momentum. Rotate to safe havens: Gold (GLD), Bonds (TLT).

↔️
Sideways

No clear direction. Mixed signals. Hold a balanced, diversified allocation across all asset types.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

Phase 1 Data Collection — DataLoader ✓ Complete

What it does: Downloads 15+ years of daily stock prices for 10 ETFs using yfinance. Also downloads economic data (VIX fear index, US Treasury yield curve, CPI inflation, unemployment) from the US Federal Reserve's FRED database.

Why this data? Price data alone isn't enough. The VIX tells us how scared the market is. The yield curve tells us if a recession might be coming. CPI tells us if inflation is high. Together, these give a complete picture of the market environment.

Smart caching: All data is saved in Parquet files so the computer doesn't have to re-download everything each time. Subsequent runs load from disk in under 1 second.

Phase 2 Feature Engineering — FeatureEngineer ✓ Complete

What it does: Transforms raw price and macro data into 15 clean, normalized signals that a machine learning model can understand. Raw prices are not useful to HMM — but metrics like "how volatile has the market been over the last 20 days compared to the last year?" are.

The 4 feature groups: Volatility (how much prices move), Momentum (direction and speed of trend), Macro (economic environment), and Cross-Asset (how different markets relate to each other).

Normalization: All 15 features are scaled using StandardScaler so no single feature dominates the model due to different units (e.g., a VIX value of 30 vs. a log return of 0.01).

Phase 3 Regime Detection — RegimeDetector (HMM) ✓ Complete

What it does: Trains a Gaussian Hidden Markov Model on the 15 normalized features to discover 3 hidden states (regimes) in the data. The model learns which patterns of features correspond to Bull, Bear, and Sideways markets — without being told explicitly.

Auto-labeling: After training, the 3 HMM states are automatically labeled. The state with the highest average daily return becomes "Bull", the lowest becomes "Bear", and the middle is "Sideways".

Smoothing: A minimum 5-day hold rule prevents the model from flip-flopping between regimes on a single noisy day. Regime changes must be consistent for at least 5 trading days.

Output: For every trading day, the model says "Bull", "Bear", or "Sideways" — with a confidence probability for each.

Phase 4 Portfolio Optimization — PortfolioOptimizer ▶ Up Next

What it does: Given the current detected regime, calculates the optimal allocation (what % to put in each of the 10 ETFs) to maximize expected return for a given level of risk. Uses the Efficient Frontier and Mean-Variance Optimization from Nobel Prize-winning Modern Portfolio Theory.

Regime-conditional: Different regimes get different target portfolios. Bull → more equities. Bear → more gold and bonds. Sideways → balanced blend.

Constraints: No single asset can exceed 30% or fall below 5% of the total portfolio, ensuring diversification at all times.

Phase 5 Backtesting — Backtester Planned

What it does: Simulates the complete strategy over 15 years of historical data to see how well it would have performed. Compares the regime-switching strategy against simply buying and holding the S&P 500 (SPY). Accounts for realistic transaction costs (0.1% per trade).

Phase 6 Live Dashboard — Streamlit App Planned

What it does: A beautiful, interactive web dashboard where you can see today's detected regime, current portfolio allocation, performance metrics, and historical regime timeline chart — all updated automatically.

Economic Indicators (FRED)

📈 VIX — Fear Index

CBOE Volatility Index. Measures how much the market expects stocks to swing. High VIX (above 30) = panic. Low VIX (below 15) = complacency. The single most important fear gauge.

🏛️ Yield Curve (10Y - 2Y)

Difference between 10-year and 2-year US Treasury yields. When this goes negative ("inverted"), it has preceded every US recession in the past 50 years.

💰 CPI — Inflation

Consumer Price Index tracks price changes over time. High, rising inflation forces the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates, which typically hurts stock valuations.

👷 Unemployment Rate

Percentage of the workforce without a job. Rising fast unemployment signals economic stress and typical accompanies Bear market conditions.

The 15 Machine Learning Features

What the HMM actually "sees" — the signals extracted from raw data to characterize market conditions.

Feature Name Group What It Measures Why It Matters
spy_log_ret Volatility Daily log return of SPY: ln(Price today / Price yesterday) Positive = market rose. Negative = market fell. Core direction signal.
spy_vol_20d Volatility Rolling 20-day realized volatility, annualized as % Spikes during crashes. Low in calm Bull markets.
spy_vol_60d Volatility Rolling 60-day realized volatility, annualized as % Background/structural volatility. Trend of vol over 3 months.
vol_ratio Volatility spy_vol_20d ÷ spy_vol_60d >1.0 = recent stress elevated vs. background. Key regime transition signal.
mom_1m_zscore Momentum 1-month cumulative return, z-scored vs. past 252 days How unusual is this month's return vs. the past year? Positive = above-trend.
mom_3m_zscore Momentum 3-month cumulative return, z-scored vs. past 252 days Medium-term trend strength. Persistence of Bull or Bear move.
mom_6m_zscore Momentum 6-month cumulative return, z-scored vs. past 252 days Long-term trend. Separates sustained Bull markets from short rallies.
vix_level Macro log(VIX) — log-transformed VIX level Log transform stabilizes spikes. The primary fear barometer.
vix_zscore Macro VIX relative to its own 252-day rolling mean/std Is fear elevated vs. its own recent history? Catches regime shifts.
yield_spread Macro 10-year minus 2-year Treasury yield Negative = inverted curve = recession signal.
yield_spread_chg Macro 20-day change in yield spread Is the curve steepening or flattening? Direction often leads equity regimes.
cpi_yoy Macro CPI year-over-year % change High rising inflation → Bear signal (Fed tightening → stock pain).
bond_equity_corr Cross-Asset 60-day rolling correlation between SPY and TLT returns Negative → flight to safety. The classic Bear marker.
commodity_trend Cross-Asset 20-day USO (oil) return, z-scored vs. 252-day history Strong oil trend → economic expansion. Collapsing oil → Bear/recession.
spy_ma_ratio Cross-Asset SPY 50-day MA ÷ SPY 200-day MA "Golden Cross" (>1.0) = Bull. "Death Cross" (<1.0) = Bear. Widely used.
Section 05

How the Hidden Markov Model Works

The AI brain of the system — explained without equations.

What Is a Hidden Markov Model?

A Hidden Markov Model (HMM) is a statistical model for situations where you observe something (like stock market data) but the true underlying state (like "Bull" or "Bear") is hidden — you can't see it directly.

💡 The analogogy: A hidden weather machine Imagine you're locked in a room with no windows. You can't see the weather outside (the hidden state), but you can observe whether people coming inside are wet or dry, wearing coats or T-shirts (the observations). Over time, you learn: when people are wet + wearing heavy coats, it's probably raining outside. The HMM does exactly this — it infers the hidden market regime from observable signals.

The Training Process (What Happens During fit())

1. Initialize

Randomly assign each day to one of 3 states (Bull/Bear/Sideways). Calculate the initial statistics of each state.

2. Expectation (E)

Given current state statistics, calculate the probability that each day belongs to each state using the Baum-Welch algorithm.

3. Maximization (M)

Update the state statistics (means and covariances) to best explain the observed probabilities. Repeat E→M until converged.

The Prediction Process (What Happens During predict())

Given a fitted model and new observations (feature matrix), the Viterbi algorithm finds the single most likely sequence of hidden states that explains the observed data. This produces one label (Bull/Bear/Sideways) per trading day.

Auto-Labeling: How States Get Named

The HMM produces states named 0, 1, 2 — it doesn't know which is Bull or Bear. After training, we look at each state's learned mean for spy_log_ret (daily return):

  • Highest mean return → Bull (markets went up more often in this state)
  • Lowest mean return → Bear (markets fell most often in this state)
  • Middle mean return → Sideways (neither strongly up nor down)

Minimum Regime Hold Smoothing

🛡️ Why this matters Raw HMM output can sometimes produce 1–2 day "blips" where the market is labeled Bull, then Bear for 2 days, then Bull again. These short transitions are almost certainly noise, not real regime changes. The 5-day minimum hold rule filters them out: a regime must persist for at least 5 consecutive trading days before it's confirmed. This dramatically improves signal quality.

Covariance Type: 'full'

We use the full covariance type, meaning each regime gets its own complete 15×15 covariance matrix. This allows the model to learn that during Bear markets, for example, VIX rising and returns falling are correlated — it captures the full statistical relationships between features per regime, not just individual feature means.

Section 07 · What's Next

Phase 4: Portfolio Optimization

The next step — using the detected regime to decide how to allocate money across the 10 ETFs.

📍 Current Position Phases 1, 2, and 3 are complete and fully tested (122 unit tests, all passing). Phase 4 is the immediate next step.

What Phase 4 Will Build

A PortfolioOptimizer class that takes the current regime as input and outputs optimal portfolio weights for the 10 ETFs. It uses PyPortfolioOpt, an industry-standard Python library implementing Harry Markowitz's Modern Portfolio Theory (Nobel Prize 1990).

Regime-Conditional Allocation Logic

🐂 Bull Regime

Target: maximize returns. Tilt heavily toward growth equities.

  • SPY, QQQ, IWM: higher weights
  • EFA, EEM: moderate
  • TLT, GLD: minimal
🐻 Bear Regime

Target: preserve capital. Rotate to safe havens and bonds.

  • TLT, GLD: higher weights
  • LQD: moderate
  • SPY, QQQ, IWM: minimal
↔️ Sideways Regime

Target: balanced growth + safety. Diversify across all.

  • All assets: moderate weights
  • Maximize Sharpe ratio
  • No strong tilt

Constraints Applied

  • Maximum position: No single ETF can exceed 30% of the portfolio
  • Minimum position: No single ETF below 5% (ensures diversification)
  • Weights sum to 100% — fully invested at all times
  • Long-only: No short selling (keeps it practical)

Optimization Objective

For Bull: maximize expected return. For Bear: minimize volatility. For Sideways: maximize Sharpe Ratio (return per unit of risk — the gold standard portfolio metric).
